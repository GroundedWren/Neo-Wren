Echoes in the Open

Prose-poetry vignettes

The dead of night is a pyroclastic surge that takes the world out of time - the smothering ash of darkness silences and stills all physical traces of humanity while the humans themselves are marked in their absence by palpable cavities in the ethereal tuff. Store lights illuminate scenes like dioramas: partially-stocked shelves, paperwork strewn about on desks, insipid lobby furniture under warehouse art, magazines and books and half-full trash cans, water fountains and vending machines and stacks of informational pamphlets - all on display behind glowing glass windows covered in ads, hand-written notices, and worn informational stenciling.

Sam’s sneakers plodded softy along the grooved white concrete of the strip mall sidewalk; their gentle impacts were the loudest sounds around, rising distinctly above the ambient hum of fluorescent lights which was barely distinguishable from her tinnitus. She realized that they signaled her presence, a timid sonic beacon in the silent void. An ancient, intrinsic instinct plucked a pizzicato tune in her brain, keeping her head on a swivel as she meandered through the calcified passages of a sleeping civilization.

Ray was counting down the exits, doing a runner’s mental math to measure his progress as his tractor-trailer rumbled down the interminable highway. Driving in the darkness of this hour gave the distinct impression of being underwater; Ray frequently imagined his little tractor cabin preserving him in a timid air bubble against the crushing pressure of the ocean as he drove through the groove of some deep-sea trench, his wheels kicking up silt as marine snow whizzed past the windows. Two exits to go; at fifty-five miles an hour, that’s 2/55 of an hour to get there, which is pretty close to 4% - with 60 minutes in an hour, 5% is 3 minutes (he’s got that one memorized), so maybe two minutes away or so. Probably.

The little radio by his seat crackled to life. “KD0UR this is KD0TZ, come back.” His heart beat a little faster, hearing that voice. It always did.

He picked up the microphone squeezed the PTT button. “KD0TZ from KD0UR; go ahead, Darryl.”

“Ray, you drive like my grandma. Where the heck are ya? I’m going to run out of sudokus at this rate.”

Ray chuckled before squeezing the microphone. “Aww, you miss me Darryl? That’s sweet. Well good news - I’m only a couple minutes away. Think you’ll survive?”

“I don’t know - you’re cutting it pretty close.”

“Always do.”

The sun set the ice-encased pine needles in the treetops ablaze in orange as a creeping blue swelled higher and higher up their trunks. Lea melted into the frigid chromatic shadows as the snow hugged her close - she wished that the treetop fires were real, were warm. She hadn’t known warmth for hours, and now may never again, she mused.

As the fire melted from the sky, she realized she wasn’t breathing and forced a shaky gulp of heavy air. She shifted uncomfortably - her limbs were barely responding now, but her snapped leg sang loudly at the provocation, its ski still attached and speared deeply into the snow.

It was supernaturally quiet here. The only sound Lea could hear was her heartbeat and breath, and both were themselves in a gradual decrescendo. I’m going to die. The thought was as devoid of fear as it was of qualification - it was simply present. A subtle shifting of the breeze blew cold against her cheeks and elucidated the outline of tears - she hadn’t realized she was crying.

The sun vanished, and Lea found eyes drawn upwards to the clear, dark sky. A million, billion stars glimmered in the perfect, unbroken void around her. She’d never seen such beauty before - they were calling to her. They were calling her by name.

A gentle snow began to fall out of the cloudless sky.

“Fuckin’ god’mn piss’uhv shit mut’ha fuck-” muttered Pat as she reached down for her fumbled keys in a pool of incandescent orange. The pads of her fingers scrabbled along the rough, dry asphalt for a moment before detecting the smooth plastic of her fob. “Fuccin’ gosha, fuckin… bish,” she slurred as she staggered back upright.

She more fell than sat into the car, and her vision took a moment to catch up to her. She blinked. Farmland spread out before her like a painting, waves of young knee-high corn broke in the foreground with the warm highlight of the bar’s light, but rapidly sank away into the purple blackness of the horizon. For a moment everything was still, even the sickly swirl of her mind.

She didn’t want to go. Not back to the hard little house firm like a splinter in the fields. Not back to the tense fuzz of the TV and its long shadows across the mismatched furniture. Not back to the Swiss-cheese walls and overflowing trash bins and rotting plates. Not back to the tangled bed of sweat and sheets, of him.

Her hand pushed the keys into the ignition with the metallic crinkle of a thimble along a washboard. What am I doing? A wave of fear poured over her, curling her toes as they pressed down on the brake. There was an unwelcome pressure in her sinuses.

I don’t want anything anymore, she realized as she twisted her hand and brought the car back to life with a gasp.

Sergio slid the glass balcony door closed behind him with what felt like more effort than he should’ve had to apply. He wished immediately that he’d brought his coat with him; they were decidedly on the winter-end of fall, and his commitment to autumnal dress was just hanging on with the last of the leaves.

She was already lighted up, the woman across the way - pretending not to notice. He pretended right back as he opened his ziplock bag and took out his half-burned joint and lighter. She took another draw from her cigarette, its red glow was a lonely firefly against the dark of her wooden apartment complex.

The wind blew gently but inconveniently as he tried to light up, sweeping over the broken fences and blackened leaves between them before ousting his nascent flames. His thumb was sore by the time he drew smoke into his mouth; his first real pull was too deep and eager (for the struggling flames’ sake) and he ended up sputtering and hacking like a bald band-aid kid who totally smokes all the time and it’s not a big deal at all thank you very much.

She was smiling at him. He couldn’t see it, not really - she was deeply silhouetted by the her roommates’ light behind her, but something about her shade told him she was smiling. He chuckled and gave her an awkward thumbs up as he leaned against his rusted railing. For a moment there was nothing but the soft sound of music somewhere deep in the building and the crunching footfalls of an unknown student walking to class below - nothing but that and the twinge of joy deep in his guts.

She took another draw; the red glow glimmered in her eyes with flecks of the same.

Jon was finding it hard to get any work done with one hand dedicated, failingly, to keeping his guts from spilling out of his abdominal cavity. His coworkers didn’t dare bring up the paperwork and IOM covered with bloody fingerprints, nor the pained groaning he couldn’t quite contain in the morning meetings - it was well known that “Jon’s having a rough go of it” and it’d really be best if his cherry-red exposed entrails went unremarked upon, at least for the first few days he was back at work.

The bloody gash through his belly hurt just as much as the day it’d been inflicted - it hurt to sit, it hurt to stand; it hurt to do nothing, and it hurt to pretend it didn’t. So fuck it, may as well have a paycheck again, he’d reasoned against everyone’s well-intentioned advice.

When lunch hour rolled around Jon found himself alone in the office park’s only gazebo with his sandwich bag; he was sheltered from the cruel cloudless sunshine but he was beginning to worry about the copious blood he’d lost to the bleachy carpets and manicured grass that day. The stupid little fountain in the middle of the man-made lake taunted him with its incessant noise and twee little form as he opened his bag.

His lunch was covered in his wife’s blood, unintentionally dripped across his chips and pastrami on rye while she’d assembled it. In the corner there was a timid note covered in her little red fingerprints.

“Jon, I know you can do it. I can tell she’s rooting for us to get through this. I love you.”

Sam meandered over the tephra-cobbled streets into darker and darker corners of the empty city, growing bolder and braver by the minute. The blackness was exciting, the silence exhilarating. She’d felt dead for so long; being out here in the real world, or at least in its corpse, filled her with a new childlike wonder. There’s no way this was safe, but that was the fucking point.

She'd unconsciously sunk into the pitch black of an empty parking lot, pulled effortlessly over the event horizon by its enveloping allure. Its gravity brought her to her knees, then lay her supine between the imperceptible paint of an American-sized parking space. The light of the city clouded the corners of the hemisphere above, but in the center there was a hole - a little aperture into the great everything beyond. She reached up and stuck her fingers through it, pulling the thin membrane of the sky apart as she at last touched the stars.
