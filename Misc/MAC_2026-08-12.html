The Salty Breeze - Chapter 5/?

During her argument with Ribbit on the snowy summit [episode 9 flashback scene], Jax came around, apologized, and later properly came out to her as transfem. The next morning, Caine visited Jax in her room and asked her if she was unhappy with the adventures. Noticing his strange behavior and fearing a negative response, Jax said they were fine. In a later discussion with the cast, they agreed that Caine was more on-edge than normal.

The next adventure was a fashion show. The cast was divided into pairs; each person would choose one outfit for themself and one for their partner. Kinger's chosen outfit for Ragatha was just her normal dress, and that got a perfect score from Caine's judge NPCs. Jax picked a punky semi-femme outfit for herself (and got some help from Ribbit), but got put into a maid outfit by Kaufmo.

After the adventure, Ragatha asked Caine if she could keep the outfit she picked for herself. He really didn't like that idea, but reluctantly agreed and stormed off. Kaufmo pressed Jax for specifics about Caine's visit to her room, but she dodged the question.

Jax, via Bubble, visited Caine in his office with the intention of getting the eyeliner pen she'd seen Ribbit use. Caine expressed anger about the cast's lack of gratitude for both the adventures and how well designed their avatars already are, but did give it to her.

Returning to the tent, Kinger (under the shroud of a hood) saw Jax with the eyeliner pen. They had an emotional conversation (dad-energy Kinger) and Jax came out to him.

The next day, Caine was absentee, and Bubble reported that he was hard at work on the next adventure which will be "special". Kaufmo, sensing danger, pressed Jax again about what Caine had said to her in her room. This turned into a big fight where Ribbit accidentally referred to Jax as "she". Even so, Jax refused to come out in the moment, and Kaufmo expressed hurt that she didn't trust him before storming off. Jax then came out to Ragatha, and they all returned to their rooms for the evening.

Jax sat in her swivel chair, feet planted on the bed and a digital paperback of Uglies cradled in her lap. She’d read the first page five times now.

Tally Youngblood was waiting for darkness.

She flipped back to the inside cover. The detail of this replica was meticulous, right down to the “missing cover” warning. It, however, did not include the name she’d clumsily scribbled in blue ink on her copy. Jax closed her eyes and tried to picture it - she could see something of the oddly unrecognizable characters, but when she tried to string them together in her mind they simply refused to coalesce. It felt like trying to force the north poles of magnets together.

She squeezed her eyes as tightly as she could, layering all her memories of the page from over the years atop each other in a single mental construct. The cloud of blue ink in the corner pooled and spotted; she was suddenly struck with the emotion of a letter. “Y”. She had a “Y” in her name! It was a good letter; probably her favorite, she realized. She felt similarly about the “X” in her name now - the end of the alphabet has style that the start simply lacks; all its chunky, buzzy mouth sounds felt like they could be succeeded by actual clicks and hums if the English language were any good.

The blue scribble of her name was always the first thing she encountered each time she read this book; it felt, strange, to not have it - like terra nova. It reminded her of just how much had changed for her so quickly in these recent months, and how much she had changed in turn. Strap in, she chuckled to herself, it’s not over yet.

It is a difficult task to be yourself, as it is only by that act that may you learn who you are. How different am I going to be? Where does the mask end, and where do I begin? Any excision can only be an approximation, but at the end of the day incisions must be made. It was a little terrifying, but also exhilarating.

She read on. Tally - oh hey, a “Y” - described how lamplight burned against the imperfections of her face. Jax, by reflex, raised a hand to her brow, sliding her fingers along her perfectly smooth forehead. She tried to picture her human face, conjuring a collage in paper mâché of reflections and cell phone photos plastered over a mannequin head. For years, she’d fixated on a score of putrid little details, massaged bones as though to whittle them down, plucked hairs and fuzz and skin like she was pulling glass out of a wound, but now that was behind her. Now, she was… whatever this is. An answer scurried into her mind as through a field of reeds: “A canvas”.

She thought back to Ribbit helping her paint her nails backstage, and to how her reflection had smiled back through the long black wig that brushed gently against her cheeks. Pretty, ugly, whatever - that felt like me. All bravery and ambition, she reached into her pocket and fetched the eyeliner. Her eyes darted around the room and a realization hit suddenly: there was no mirror. There never had been.

Jax deflated and idly rubbed her arm where Ribbit had squeezed it, releasing the lingering affection into her blood flow (or some simulation thereof). Ribbit will help me, just like she did with the nail polish. Not like I’d know what I’m doing yet anyway, even if I had a mirror. Jax stood to go find her and briefly mused about slipping a heater under her sheets before sneaking out, just like Tally. It occurred to her that Caine might in fact be watching, but that fear was not enough to keep her from going through the door.

It felt clandestine to walk down the dimly lighted hall; she stepped as quietly as she could as she made her way past Kaufmo’s door, then paused as a whining, cyclical tune caught her ear. The electric, woody hum grew louder and clearer as she approached Ribbit’s room; at the inception of a new iteration of the progression, a high, tremulous, whistled melody joined the lone instrument. Ribbit was playing the violin she’d convinced Caine to make for her - Jax had seen it once or twice, but Ribbit didn’t really ever bring it out of her room.

The tune was simple, sad, and quite hypnotic. Jax allowed herself to be arrested by it; she sat on the floor and closed her eyes, letting the sonic waves wash over her. Ribbit’s talent was apparent, and she conducted emotion elegantly into the piece - the shrill highs of her whistles and the low fry of the strings sent chills down Jax’s spine. She could’ve sat there for hours.

Eventually, Ribbit concluded with a long, minor note that decrescendoed into a delicate, lingering vibration as one last whistle drained bloodless from her tongue. After a pause, Jax shakily exhaled and stood to approach the door.

Her fist froze just short of the wood. Does she still need space? Her hand fell to her side, and she tapped her foot anxiously. I don’t want to ice her out like before, either. Maybe reaching out will mean something. She raised her hand again, but couldn’t seem to begin the motion.

“You can come in if you want to come in, bunny,” Ribbit said quietly.

Jax blushed and sucked her teeth. She had a strange urge to run away, but she swallowed it, put her hand on the knob, and slowly opened the door.
