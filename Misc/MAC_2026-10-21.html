The corridor smelled strongly of many things - wet dog, plug-in chemical air fresheners, onions simmered between one and three days ago, long-dried piss, and weed - always weed. Luke didn’t mind much, though - deep in this dingy maze of an apartment complex was Frank’s place, all of his friends, and a lot of alcohol; the sensory challenge of getting there was easily eclipsed by the joy of partying hard into the night. It was perhaps two in the morning, and it was always around this time that Luke would’ve had enough drinks to start to actually relax and open up, to come out of his nervous shell and not care quite so much about how his friends - how the world saw him.

They were on a mission, he and Frank, to fetch their miraculously arrived and hopefully correct delivery order from the local burger place. The mid-party meal was always a welcome interlude; the process of hunting/gathering the sloppily assembled cuisine and its subsequent consumption always shifted things down a couple gears and brought the raucouteers back into their own heads for a quick respite (to be followed by an inevitable second wind). Plus, it wasn’t often that he got to be alone with Frank. His vibe, his scent was muddled in with the others’ while they were in the apartment, but out here the stench of the hallway didn’t stand a chance - it was all him.

“Hey Franky,” Luke said, his words moving as slowly as his brain, “you never told me how your date went yesterday. So, y’know,” he lazily flipped his hand around by his tilted head, “how was it?”

Frank made a throaty noise and smiled, “Oh yeah, I didn’t, did I? Well, there’s nothing to tell really - dude didn’t look like his pictures and we didn’t vibe. You know how it goes, man.” He pressed the plastic elevator button which illuminated in a yellow that was probably supposed to be white. “The apps suck.”

“Yeahhh,” said Luke, unsure what emotion he was going for, “that uh, sucks. Like you said.”

“No big,” said Frank as the elevator dinged feebly, “I hadn’t gotten my hopes up this time yet. I’m learning, you see.”

The elevator ride down was, to Luke, a slightly uncomfortable silence. Frank, on the other hand, didn’t appear to notice. Luke was able to focus on that - to let Frank’s cool wash over him and ease his nerves. The tightness deep inside him didn’t really let go, though; its cold talons were wrapped loosely but threateningly around his entrails.

The food was waiting patiently by the entryway - thank god - and it smelled wonderfully greasy. Luke could taste it already - his mouth salivating in anticipation even as the fingers in his guts were slowly closing in, tighter and tighter.

The ride up was unbearably silent - he had to say something. “Hey so,” he started, and put his hand up to his neck as he lost the nerve to continue and trailed off.

“What’s up?” asked Frank as he turned to look at him with his piercingly dark eyes. He had a friendly smile on his thin lips while the hazy fluorescence made his brown hair shine like gold.

Fuck. “Uh,” Luke clicked his tongue nervously, “so, you’re like, gay, right?”

Frank laughed and leaned against the wall. “What tipped you off?”

“Tch”, Luke chuckled uncomfortably. “I uh, I was just curious,” he began, his heart pounding, “have you like, ever been attracted to girls, like at all?”

The elevator rasped its arrival chime weakly and the doors slid open. Frank just looked at him a moment, his eyes glimmering in a way that reminded Luke, for some reason, of volcanic glass. “Uh,” he said after a moment and cracked a smile, “I mean, maybe? I had a couple ‘crushes’ back in like, middle school, but now,” he jammed his foot against the elevator door as it started to close, “I’m not sure. I hadn’t really thought about it. Why do you ask?”

Luke stepped out of the elevator and started down the hall, Frank in tow. “I dunno, just a random thought. I kinda struggle to imagine what it’s like not to be bisexual, sometimes.”

“Ah,” said Frank, “I see. Well, I mean, I can appreciate women aesthetically, I guess - I just, haven’t felt the pull, you know?”

“Yeah,” muttered Luke - they’d come up to the closed apartment door. Its dark wood contained the party noises within surprisingly well - only a faint thumping of music reverberated out through the walls. Frank cracked it open and the joyful noises of revelry spilled out, washing over and swirling around them. When he took a step inside, the cold fingers through Luke’s guts squeezed tight, forcing a vocal protest from him.

“B-but,” Luke found himself saying, catching his friend’s attention. “Like, not even a little? Like, out of a hundred - zero zero?” The world stood still.

“Yeah, man, zero, I think.”

No response.

“Come on, Luke, they’re waiting for us,” he coaxed.

Quinn was celebrating - finding gainful employment was no small feat these days - but the festivities that night were mostly designed to thank his pal, Leo, for the assistance. The usual five suspects to be his friends had turned up and were well into a night of bowling and drinking - this was courtesy of ONAN Business Solutions, Inc (ONANBSI), via Quinn’s signing bonus. It wasn’t just Leo’s glowing recommendation that earned him the job, as Quinn quite hoped he’d’ve been able to get it anyway, but that favor was the continuation of a long pattern of little things Quinn had to be grateful for from his friend. He’d kept those favors like trinkets, tucked them safely away in that dusty little box in your mind where you hide the things you cherish, and now he felt the need to share with his friend how much they meant to him - how much he meant to him.

It was plain to see that Leo was having a good time; he was laughing and joking and beaming that big smile of his all around, bathing them all in light. What a good evening, mused Quinn.

Strike, spare, gutter - the gang’s scores slumped into the sub-90s while, coincidentally, Quinn was beginning to notice a slight spin to the room. He stood and reached into his jacket pocket to shake a pack of smokes at his friend - Come with me.

It was a warm, muggy night that buzzed loudly with bug-song, even here with an expanse of dead parking lot between them and the trees. The thickness of the air bore down together with the deep darkness of space; the blackness was only kept from completely swallowing the ground by some low-pressure sodium-vapor lamps. It was the kind of night that made you feel underwater.

Lighting the cigarette gave Quinn the distinct impression of starting a campfire, building a warm little haven for the two of them amid the blackness. He drew and felt his head steady, his muscles easing into calmness as he relaxed against the metal railing. I’ve done this before. I know what I’m doing. I’m fine. I’m fine.

“Nice night,” he said as he held out the cigarette for his friend. He meant the words, but all he really meant was that he wanted to talk.

Leo reached over and took the cig, brushing Quinn’s lithe fingers incidentally. “Yeah,” he said in a low voice before taking a drag; after the exhale, he smiled and looked over at his friend, “Congrats again, man. You really earned this job. I meant what I said in that letter, you know - you’re gonna kill it.”

Quinn chuckled. “You think? You’re really too kind to me, Leo.”

“I’m just stating facts.”

He’s into this. You’re fine. Go for it. Quinn tilted his head a bit, looking warmly down at his friend. “Thank you. I very much appreciate you. I, uh,” his lips twitched in a smile, “quite admire you, in fact.”

“Huh,” Leo laughed warmly as he passed the cigarette back, “thanks, Quinn.” Their fingers brushed again.

Calmly, Quinn, calmly, he thought as he raised his trembling hand to his mouth. He drew slowly, then leaned slightly over to lay his hand atop Leo’s, feeling his warmth and the dark hair on his wrist. He forgot to exhale.

Leo’s eyes widened slightly and his lips parted in surprise - impossible to read, infuriating surprise. No? Yes? YES?

“Uh, Quinn, uh, I’m sorry,” he said with audible sadness, “you’re great, I love you man, but I’m not into guys. I’m just,” he winced, “I’m sorry.”

Quinn swallowed the smoke - it was somehow cold. Okay. Okay. Easy. “Ah, of course. I, uh,” he retracted his hand, “I’m sorry if that was uncomfortable.” No tears. It’s fine. It’s fine.

“Psh,” he said, cracking that famous smile back open, “not at all; hell of a complement. You’re a good friend, man.”

“Yeah,” Quinn smiled and exhaled, “you, too.”
