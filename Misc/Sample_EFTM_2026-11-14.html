Prose vignettes

Nicholas P. Rabinowitz tossed a dulled tennis ball up into the cool autumn air and snatched it back at its apex, denying it a natural decline back down to Earth. He barely even noticed its feeling in his hands, having spent what was probably years, cumulatively, with such a ball in his left hand - at times it felt strange to not be idly futzing with one.

Desiccating leaves crunched under the once-white soles of his tennis shoes - shoes which had served him well but which were now worn only ceremoniously, a memento from the days he’d aspired to join the Show, the very thing which he was now en route to watch on the good ol’ teleputer at his friend Damian’s place (who’d shelled out for the primo InterLace dissemination bandwidth so as to be able to stream two games at once without breaking a sweat or even a remarkable three with acceptable degradation). Damian also had these kick-ass leather bowl chairs which were not only comfortable but also super classy and fung shui and shit. He very much wanted a set of his own, but as they were a bit of a specialty item that hadn’t yet reappeared at any Pier 1 within reasonable radius he only got to experience these wonders of Pakistanian craftsmanship when he had occasion to visit the D-Man.

N.P.R. was thinking about what good taste in furniture his friend had as he (Nick) knocked on the door to his (Damian’s) apartment; his knuckles rapped on a spot which had just about started to show ware from all his visits over the years. They’d been friends even while Nick was an E.T.A. kid, having met at the Unexamined Life after a particularly devastating loss to his P.W.T.A Boys’ B-4 18s counterpart 3-5, 7-5, 5-7 at the annual E.T.A.-P.W. rivalry thing way back in the Year of the Tucks Medicated Pad.

The door swung open and Damy immediately handed him a pre-opened Bud Light Lime, a beverage known to be beloathed by Nick “Manly Drinks” Rabinowitz.

“The fuck is this, man?” he whinged as he held the can at arm’s length, “you trying to ward me off like a Dracula or some shit?”

“I bid thee, invited, cross the threshold into my home,” Damy said as he flashed his bright but yet blunt-toothed grin at his friend, “the WhataBurger’s about to start.”

Jim Troeltsch could be heard from the entryway as Nicky P.R. doffed his shoes - he (Troeltsch) was going on about all the new up-and-coming players from around the northeast, carefully giving due focus to those either clearly Show-bound or for whom the coverage at S.P.N. had been able - by pure circumstance - to construct some kind-of hero or villain narrative around and successfully sold to the viewership as a Real Life Story. It was these stories that Damy seemed most interested in, Nick had noted, but for him the focus was on the Sport, on the Athleticism. It was nice, though, in a utopian kind of thinking, that Damy was so interested in his fellow humans, so invested in experiencing the perspectives of others. This was something that Nick had come to realize he was missing through knowing his friend, something cut out of him - it was like waking up on a cold surgical table and finding an expertly sutured incision along your abdomen. No, it was more like running your fingers along a faded scar you’ve seemingly always had and, for the first time, feeling the prickly tip of an undissolved stitch. A part of him was gone.

Nick poured the gifted Bud Light Lime down its bestower’s sink in a sputtering, fizzing stream. Through a grin he asked Damian where he kept the real drinks, and Damy was all to happy to produce a ¾ full bottle of high-brow (yet accessible) rye.The duo were all laughs andgaiety as the portions were portioned - it was a comfortable, safe dynamic. Nick enjoyed it. Enjoyed how easy it was. This was a social situation where they both knew exactly what to do and were entirely on the same page, where each felt affection enough for the other to offer them conversational grace and beneficiating of the doubt. Nick could laugh weird or not get a joke or go off on a ramble or (god forbid) be wrong about something and it was okay - it was always okay with the D-man.

They were seated in the bowl chairs, ice clinking against their whiskey glasses as the dual T.P viewers displayed (in stunning 480p) the Y.O.H.W.A.C.C WhataBurger Southwest Junior Invitational’s Boys’ 18s singles feature match aside the famous S.P.N. sports-casting desk of Jim Troeltsch (featuring play-by-play caster C. Olor), when Nicholas began to rub at the aching psychic scar on his abdomen like he was Harry Potter or something. It was times like this when the phantom pain of whatever had been taken was at its worst, when he could practically conjure his missing flesh back into place, raw and skinless and and beating as it was. He cared about Damy - he felt an affinity for him above and beyond what was due of even such a close friend. It wasn’t about numbers or logic or greasing the wheels or acting in accordance with axiom, no, he cared about Damian more than “made sense,” more than he should, and he couldn’t help it.

As the night went on they tuned into games from the Show proper and sort-of let them fade into the background noise as they started up and got lost in chatting. Damy wanted to know if he felt any nostalgia for E.T.A. or for the doing of the sport at such a high level despite The Horrors he’d slowly gotten him (Nick) to divulge through providing conversational space and a level-headed ear, emotionally. Nick didn’t know. He’d probably guess so, though, despite the fact that it felt like a self-betrayal to admit. He wanted to hate E.T.A., to be able to look back on it with complete rejection or not at all, to keep it as far away as he could. Still, he’d felt some camaraderie with his fellow E.T.A.s. He’d gotten to play a lot of the beautiful game or Tennis, distilled and concentrated to bitterness though it was. He got to feel the euphoria of pushing his body just a little too hard and forever changing a little part of himself, a little corner of his brain brain, time and time again. It was bad, but it felt good - and it was good, but it felt bad.

Damian just nodded. “Complicated, huh?”

“Yeah, complicated.”

“You know, I always had a different picture in my head of what being among best in the world at something meant.”

“I wasn’t the best in the world.”

“I thought it meant, like, confidence, self-assurance, and ease.”

“Ease?”

“I know, right? Shows what I knew. I’ve just, never had that kind of talent. I’m good at things, don’t get me wrong, but I’ve always felt, I don’t know, commonplace.”

“Fuck off, ‘commonplace.’”

“What?”

“I, uh,” he stood from the bowl, “dunno.” He walked over to the fridge and pulled its magnetized door open. “Want anything?”

“No, I’m good,” Damy replied, still holding a mostly full portion of rye (his second).

Nick looked around the fridge a moment, focusing more on looking like he was looking for something than on actually finding anything or coming up with something suitable to say.

“It doesn’t define you, you know,” said Damy in the soft but well-projected tone he uses when he’s saying something he thinks you don’t really believe.

Nick closed the fridge and leaned against the island. “What doesn’t?”

“Come on, Nicky,” he said as he stood, “Pay attention. I’m trying to tell you that your value isn’t tied to your abilities and other sappy stuff like that. I could go on,” he paused to take a sip as he walked over to the island, “for hours about humanist values and the healing power of self-acceptance and how I understand that those things aren’t easy for you, but I think it’d be more productive to get right to the heart of what I mean.” He set his drink on the table and set his hands firmly onto Nick’s shoulders. “What I mean is that I care about you, man. And I don’t care about your ability as a tennis player or an accountant or a joke-teller or anything else beyond how those things shape who you are.”

“…woah.”
