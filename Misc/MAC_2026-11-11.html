Nicholas P. Rabinowitz tossed a bright-yellow tennis ball up into the cool autumn air and snatched back it at its apex, denying it a natural decline back down to Earth. He barely even noticed its feeling in his hands, having spent what was probably years1, cumulatively, with such a ball in his left hand - at times it felt strange to not be idly futzing with one.

Brown, curling leaves crunched under the white soles of his well-worn tennis shoes - shoes which had served him well but which were now worn only ceremoniously, a memento from the days he’d aspired to join the Show, a Show which he was now en route to watch on the good ol’ teleputer at his friend Damian’s place (he’d shelled out for the primo InterLace dissemination bandwidth and could stream two games at once without breaking a sweat and three with only minor degradation). Damian also had these kick-ass black-leather bowl chairs which were not only extremely comfortable but also super classy and fung shui and shit. He very much wanted a set of his own, but as they were a bit of a specialty item that hadn’t yet reappeared at any Pier 1 in a reasonable radius he only got to experience these wonders of Pakistanian craftsmanship when he had occasion to visit the D-Man.

N.P.R. was thinking about what good taste in furniture his friend had as he (Nick) knocked on the door to his (Damian’s) apartment, his knuckles rapping on a spot which had just about started to show ware from all his visits over the years. They’d been friends even while Nick was an E.T.A. man, having met at the Unexamined Life after a particularly devastating loss to his P.W.T.A Boys’ B-4 18s counterpart 6-3, 5-7, 7-5 at the annual E.T.A.-P.W. rivalry thing way back in the Year of the Tucks Medicated Pad2.

The door swung open and Damy immediately handed him a cold and pre-opened Bud Light Lime, a beverage known to be beloathed by Nick “Big Manly Drinks” Rabinowitz.

“The fuck is this, man?” he whinged3 as he held the can at arm’s length, “you trying to ward me off like a Dracula or some shit?”

“I bid thee, invited, cross the threshold into my home,” Damy said as he flashed his signature bright but yet blunt-toothed grin at his friend, “The WhataBurger’s4 about to start.”

Jim Troeltsch could be heard from the entryway as N.P.R. doffed his shoes - he was going on about all the new up-and-coming players from around the northeast, carefully giving due focus to those either clearly Show-bound or for whom the coverage at S.P.N.6 had been able - by pure circumstance - construct some kind-of hero or villain narrative around and successfully sold to the viewership as a Real Life Story7. It was these stories that Damy seemed most interested in, Nick had noted, but for him the focus was on the Sport, on the Athleticism9.
